Malawi Digital Plant Health Service

A National Digital Platform for Plant Health and Agriculture

The Malawi Digital Plant Health Service (MaDiPHS) is a national digital agricultural plant health service designed to bring together data, knowledge, analytical tools and digital services to support better plant health and agricultural decision-making in Malawi.

The MaDiPHS National Client provides a locally adapted interface through which farmers, extension workers, researchers, agricultural professionals and decision makers can access agricultural information, monitor plant-health risks and use digital tools to support informed decisions.

What is MaDiPHS?

MaDiPHS is designed to establish a national digital agricultural plant health service for Malawi. It brings together information and digital systems that can help identify, monitor, manage and predict agricultural pests and diseases.

Rather than operating agricultural information systems independently, MaDiPHS provides an environment where relevant national, regional and international data, knowledge and digital tools can contribute to a common national service.

The National Client is intended to translate this information into services that are useful to Malawian farmers, extension personnel, researchers and agricultural decision makers.

Our Goal

To provide a digital service that supports targeted and efficient management of pests and diseases affecting important crops in Malawi.

Our Approach

To connect agricultural data, scientific knowledge and digital technologies and translate them into practical information for agricultural decision-making.

A Collaborative National Service

MaDiPHS is built around collaboration between Malawian institutions and national and international partners. The service brings together existing digital plant-health systems, data assets and agricultural knowledge rather than treating each system as an isolated source of information.

The approach supports local adaptation and co-production so that digital services respond to the needs of Malawian farmers, extension workers, researchers and agricultural decision makers.
